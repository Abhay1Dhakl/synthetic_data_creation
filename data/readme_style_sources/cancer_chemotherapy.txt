Introduction
Chemotherapy, also called chemo, is a type of cancer treatment that uses drugs to kill cancer cells.
It works by killing or stopping the growth of cancer and other fast-growing cells.
Chemotherapy can be used to cure cancer, reduce the chance that it will return, or stop or slow its growth.
It can also be used to shrink tumors that are causing pain or other problems.

Role in Cancer Care
Chemotherapy is used to treat many types of cancer.
For some people, chemotherapy may be the only treatment they receive.
More often, chemotherapy is used together with other cancer treatments.
When used with other treatments, chemotherapy may make a tumor smaller before surgery or radiation therapy.
It may also destroy cancer cells that remain after surgery or radiation therapy.
Chemotherapy can help other treatments work better.
It can also be used when cancer has returned or spread to other parts of the body.

How Chemotherapy Is Given
Chemotherapy can be given in many ways.
Some medicines are taken by mouth as pills, capsules, or liquids.
Some are given by intravenous infusion directly into a vein.
Some are given by injection into a muscle or under the skin.
Other methods include intrathecal, intraperitoneal, intra-arterial, or topical treatment depending on the clinical situation.
IV chemotherapy is often given through a thin needle in a vein in the hand or lower arm.

Catheters, Ports, and Pumps
A catheter is a thin, soft tube placed into a large vein, often in the chest area.
Catheters can also be used to give other drugs and to draw blood.
A port is a small round disc placed under the skin during minor surgery before treatment begins.
A catheter connects the port to a large vein.
A nurse can insert a needle into the port to give chemotherapy or draw blood.
Pumps can be attached to catheters or ports to control how much and how fast chemotherapy enters the body.
Pumps may be internal or external.

Choosing Treatment
There are many different chemotherapy drugs.
The treatment plan depends on the type of cancer, how advanced it is, whether chemotherapy has been used before, and whether the patient has other health problems.

Side Effects
Chemotherapy affects fast-growing healthy cells as well as cancer cells.
Healthy cells in the mouth, intestines, and hair are common examples.
Side effects may include mouth sores, nausea, and hair loss.
Side effects often improve or go away after chemotherapy finishes.
Fatigue is one of the most common side effects.

Supportive Care
Patients may need help planning for fatigue on the day of treatment and the day after treatment.
It may help to arrange a ride to and from chemotherapy.
It may also help to plan time for rest and ask for help with meals or childcare.
There are many ways to manage chemotherapy side effects, and patients should talk with their care team about them.

Treatment Setting and Cost
Chemotherapy may be received at home, in a clinic, in a doctor's office, or during a hospital stay.
The cost depends on the types of drugs used, the doses, how often treatment is given, and where treatment takes place.
Patients may need to talk with their insurance provider and treatment center about coverage and financial support.
