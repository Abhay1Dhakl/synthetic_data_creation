Introduction
Glaucoma is a group of eye diseases that can cause vision loss and blindness.
It damages the optic nerve at the back of the eye.
The symptoms can start so slowly that a person may not notice them.
There is no cure for glaucoma, but early treatment can often stop the damage and protect vision.

Types
There are many types of glaucoma.
The most common type in the United States is open-angle glaucoma.
Other types include angle-closure glaucoma and congenital glaucoma.

Symptoms
At first, glaucoma usually has no symptoms.
About half of people with glaucoma do not know they have it.
Over time, side vision may be lost slowly.
Without treatment, glaucoma can eventually cause blindness.

Risk
Anyone can get glaucoma, but some people have a higher risk.
Risk is higher in people over age 60, especially Hispanic or Latino adults.
Risk is also higher in African American adults over age 40.
A family history of glaucoma raises risk.
People at higher risk should ask how often they need to be checked.

Emergency Symptoms
Angle-closure glaucoma can cause sudden intense eye pain.
It can also cause nausea, a red eye, and blurry vision.
These symptoms need urgent medical attention right away.

Diagnosis
The only way to know whether a person has glaucoma is to get a comprehensive dilated eye exam.
Eye doctors can check for glaucoma during a simple and painless dilated eye exam.
The doctor uses eye drops to widen the pupil and then checks for glaucoma and other eye problems.
The exam includes a visual field test to check side vision.

Important Facts
Glaucoma can happen in one eye or both eyes.
Some people with high eye pressure do not get glaucoma.
There is also a type of glaucoma that happens in people with normal eye pressure.
Normal eye pressure is different from person to person.

Treatment
Treatment options include medicines, usually eye drops, laser treatment, and surgery.
Treatment should start right away after diagnosis.
Treatment cannot undo vision already lost, but it can help stop the damage from getting worse.
Prescription eye drops are the most common treatment.
Laser treatment can help fluid drain out of the eye.
If medicines and laser treatment do not work, surgery may be recommended.

Follow-up and Counseling
If a doctor prescribes medicine, it should be used every day as directed.
Patients should tell their doctor if treatment causes side effects.
Regular check-ups are important.
If vision loss affects daily activities, vision rehabilitation services or devices may help.
Family members may also need to get checked because glaucoma can run in families.

Prevention and Screening
There is no sure way to prevent glaucoma.
That is why eye exams are important.
A dilated eye exam is one of the best ways to detect eye disease early before it causes vision loss.
Many people with diabetes or high blood pressure need a dilated eye exam at least once a year.
