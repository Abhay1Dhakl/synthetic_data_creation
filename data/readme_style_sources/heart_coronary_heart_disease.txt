Introduction
Heart disease is a general term for conditions that affect the heart's structure and how it works.
Coronary heart disease is a type of heart disease that happens when the heart's arteries cannot deliver enough oxygen-rich blood to the heart muscle.
This usually happens because fatty deposits called plaque build up inside the coronary arteries.
Coronary heart disease is also called coronary artery disease or ischemic heart disease.
It is the most common type of heart disease.
For many people, coronary heart disease is preventable with a heart-healthy lifestyle and medicines.

Symptoms
Symptoms vary from person to person.
Some people do not know they have coronary heart disease until they develop chest pain, a heart attack, or sudden cardiac arrest.
As plaque builds up, a person may notice chest pain, shortness of breath, or pain in the neck during activity.
These symptoms may improve with rest.
Heart attack pain may feel like pressure, squeezing, tightness, or burning in the chest.
The discomfort may spread to the shoulders, arms, neck, jaw, or back.
Cold sweats, dizziness, nausea, shortness of breath, and weakness can also happen during a heart attack.
If it is not clear whether chest pain is a heart attack, emergency care should be sought right away.

Risk Factors
Risk rises as people get older.
A family history of early heart disease increases risk.
High blood pressure, high LDL cholesterol, and smoking are major risk factors.
Diabetes, obesity, physical inactivity, poor sleep, and chronic stress also increase risk.
Long-term exposure to secondhand smoke can damage blood vessels.
Unhealthy eating patterns can contribute to plaque buildup in the heart's arteries.

Diagnosis
Healthcare providers diagnose coronary heart disease using symptoms, medical history, family history, risk factors, and heart tests.
The condition may be obstructive, nonobstructive, or related to disease in the heart's small blood vessels.
Women may be harder to diagnose quickly when the disease is nonobstructive.

Treatment
Treatment depends on how serious the symptoms are and what other health conditions are present.
Treatment may include heart-healthy lifestyle changes, medicines, procedures, or surgery.
Heart-healthy eating plans include fruits, vegetables, and whole grains and limit saturated fat, sodium, added sugars, and alcohol.
Routine physical activity can help manage high cholesterol, high blood pressure, overweight, and other risk factors.
Quitting smoking is important because smoking damages and tightens blood vessels.
Getting enough quality sleep and managing stress are also part of treatment.

Medicines
Some medicines reduce chest pain or help the heart work with less force.
ACE inhibitors, beta blockers, and angiotensin-receptor blockers can lower blood pressure.
Calcium channel blockers allow blood vessels to relax.
Nitrates such as nitroglycerin can relieve or prevent angina.
Statins help control high cholesterol and slow plaque buildup.
Some people may also need blood thinners or aspirin, depending on their risk and bleeding history.

Procedures
Percutaneous coronary intervention, also called coronary angioplasty, can open narrowed or blocked coronary arteries.
A stent may be placed to help keep the artery open.
Coronary artery bypass grafting improves blood flow to the heart by routing blood around blocked arteries.
CABG is often used for severe coronary artery disease that affects multiple arteries.

CABG Recovery
After CABG, many people stay in the hospital for about one week.
Some patients spend a day or two in an intensive care unit after surgery.
Medical staff may monitor heart rhythm, give oxygen, control pain, and help prevent blood clots.
After leaving the hospital, full recovery may take about 6 to 12 weeks.
Common short-term side effects include chest pain near the surgical cut, constipation, poor appetite, mood changes, sleep problems, tiredness, and swelling where the graft was removed.
Cardiac rehabilitation can help recovery by combining supervised exercise, education, and counseling.
If signs of a heart attack happen after surgery, urgent medical care is needed immediately.
