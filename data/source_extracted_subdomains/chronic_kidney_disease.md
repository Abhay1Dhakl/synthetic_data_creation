# Chronic Kidney Disease

## Block 1

Source title: Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) - NIDDK  
Source URL: https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/chronic-kidney-disease-ckd

Important extracted text:

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) means your kidneys are damaged or have a problem with their structure and can't filter blood the way they should.

Key risk factors are diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease, and a family history of kidney disease.

Diabetes and high blood pressure are the most common causes of chronic kidney disease in adults.

Testing may be the only way to know if you have kidney disease.

The sooner you know you have kidney disease, the sooner you can get treatment.

## Block 2

Source title: Managing Chronic Kidney Disease - NIDDK  
Source URL: https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/chronic-kidney-disease-ckd/managing

Important extracted text:

The most important step you can take to treat kidney disease is to control your blood pressure.

Ten ways to manage kidney disease:

- Control your blood pressure
- Meet your blood glucose goal if you have diabetes
- Work with your health care team to monitor your kidney health
- Take medicines as prescribed
- Work with a dietitian to develop a meal plan
- Make physical activity part of your routine
- Aim for a healthy weight
- Get enough sleep
- Stop smoking
- Find healthy ways to cope with stress and depression

The tests that health care providers use to test for kidney disease can also be used to track changes to kidney function and damage.

## Block 3

Source title: Preventing Chronic Kidney Disease - NIDDK  
Source URL: https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/chronic-kidney-disease-ckd/prevention

Important extracted text:

You are more likely to develop kidney disease if you have:

- Diabetes
- High blood pressure
- Heart disease
- A family history of kidney failure

Early kidney disease may not have any symptoms, so getting tested may be the only way to know your kidneys are healthy.

Choose foods that are healthy for your heart and your entire body.

Aim for less than 2,300 milligrams of sodium each day.

Be active for 30 minutes or more on most days.

## Block 4

Source title: Healthy Eating for Adults with Chronic Kidney Disease - NIDDK  
Source URL: https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/chronic-kidney-disease-ckd/healthy-eating-adults-chronic-kidney-disease

Important extracted text:

When you have chronic kidney disease (CKD), the kidneys can't filter your blood the way they should.

Choosing healthy foods and beverages, and avoiding foods high in sodium, potassium, and phosphorus, may prevent or delay some health problems from CKD.

People with CKD may need to limit how much liquid they consume because damaged kidneys can't remove extra fluid.

Too much phosphorus in your blood can harm your blood vessels and make your bones thin, weak, and more likely to break.

Having too little or too much potassium in your blood can cause heart and muscle problems.

Too much sodium causes your blood to retain fluids. When your body retains fluids, it can cause high blood pressure and swelling, putting stress on your kidneys and heart.

## Block 5

Source title: Chronic Kidney Disease Tests & Diagnosis - NIDDK  
Source URL: https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/chronic-kidney-disease-ckd/tests-diagnosis

Important extracted text:

Early kidney disease usually doesn't have any symptoms. Testing is the only way to know how well your kidneys are working.

To check for kidney disease, health care providers use:

- A blood test that checks how well your kidneys are filtering your blood, called GFR
- A urine test to check for albumin

The results of the test mean the following:

- A GFR of 60 or more is in the normal range.
- A GFR of less than 60 may mean you have kidney disease.
- A GFR of 15 or less is called kidney failure.

If you have kidney disease, your health care provider will use the same two tests to help monitor your kidney disease and make sure your treatment plan is working.

## Block 6

Source title: Causes of Chronic Kidney Disease in Adults - NIDDK  
Source URL: https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/chronic-kidney-disease-ckd/causes

Important extracted text:

The most common causes of chronic kidney disease in adults are diabetes and high blood pressure.

High blood glucose levels can damage your kidneys' filters.

High blood pressure can damage blood vessels and other parts of the filtering system in the kidneys.

Other causes of CKD in adults include:

- Acute kidney injury
- Certain cancers and related conditions
- Disorders in which the body's immune system attacks its own cells and organs
- Drugs that are toxic to the kidneys
- Glomerular diseases
- Infections
- Kidney stones with complications
- Metabolic syndrome
- Polycystic kidney disease
- Severe obesity
- Sickle cell disease
